Take Action

Your neighborhood needs you in the room.

Zoning amendments don't pass themselves. They pass because residents show up, write letters, and make the case in public. Here's exactly what to do and how to do it.

The Five Actions That Move This

01

Show up at the ARB hearing.

The Architectural Review Board is the first stop for any HC-G2 text amendment. The ARB meets monthly and agendas are posted at norfolkva.gov. When the application is filed, there will be a public comment period. Your presence matters — three neighbors saying "I support this" carries more weight than you'd expect.

You don't need to be a lawyer or a planner. Say your name, your address, and one concrete thing you want: "I want to be able to get coffee on Colonial Avenue without driving to Colley." That's enough.

02

Show up at the Planning Commission hearing.

After the ARB, the amendment goes to a public hearing before the Planning Commission. This is where the planning staff presents their analysis and residents testify. Same instructions: show up, say your name and address, keep it short, be specific. A full room tells the commission this isn't a fringe request.

The Planning Commission meets at City Hall, 810 Union Street. Check norfolkva.gov/planning for agendas and dates once the application is filed.

03

Write a letter to your City Council member.

HC-G2 falls in Norfolk's Superward 6. A brief, direct letter from a constituent carries real weight — especially from a property owner. You don't need to write a policy brief. You need to make it personal: what do you want on your block, and why does the current rule get in the way?

Use the template below or write your own. Email is fine. Mailed letters are even better.

04

Print zines and talk to neighbors.

Zine No. 1 — "Why Is Your Street Dead?" — is licensed for free printing. Print 10 copies, leave them at the coffee shop, drop them in mailboxes on your block, bring them to the next civic league meeting. The goal is to make this legible to people who haven't heard of HC-G2.

Download the print-ready PDF from the Zines section. Letter-size, prints on any home printer.

05

Talk to the Ghent Civic League.

The Ghent Civic League is the organized neighborhood voice. If the League endorses the amendment, it changes the political calculus at City Hall. If you're a member — or can attend a meeting — bring this campaign to the table. A presentation offer from a former board member of the historic district is hard to turn down.

The League meets at the Ghent United Methodist Church. Check their calendar at ghentciviclague.org or ask a neighbor.

Neighbor Letter Template

Copy, personalize, and send.

Address to your City Council member (Superward 6) or to the Planning Commission. Include your name, address, and the phrase "HC-G2 text amendment" so staff can route it correctly.

Dear [Council Member / Planning Commission Chair], My name is [Your Name], and I live at [Your Address] in the Ghent HC-G2 historic district. I am writing to express my support for the proposed text amendment to Table 3.6.11 of the Norfolk Zoning Ordinance that would permit neighborhood-scale retail uses by right on ground-floor street-facing parcels in HC-G2. Ghent has exceptional bones — dense, walkable, beautifully maintained — but our sidewalks are dead. The current zoning code requires a Conditional Use Permit for any ground-floor retail use, which means a $1,080 city application fee plus legal and planning consultants — totaling $3,000–$8,000 in typical process costs — a six-month wait, and a public hearing. No small operator can afford that process. The result is what we have now: two nonconforming corporate stores, vacant storefronts, and no neighborhood retail by right anywhere in the district. Norfolk already knows how to solve this. The Pedestrian Commercial Overlay on Colley Avenue and 21st Street uses performance standards instead of CUPs to permit small-scale retail while maintaining neighborhood character. It works. We're asking that the same model be applied to HC-G2. This is not a request to override historic preservation standards. ARB design review for physical alterations would remain in place. We are asking only that the use table be updated to reflect what our neighborhood actually needs. I urge your support for this amendment. Sincerely, [Your Name] [Your Address] [Your Email]
