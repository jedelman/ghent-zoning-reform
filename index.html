The Problem

Historic protection shouldn't mean commercial prohibition.

Norfolk's HC-G2 district was designed to protect Ghent's historic residential fabric — and it does. But it also prohibits virtually all street-facing commercial activity by right. No café, no corner store, no bookshop, no neighborhood bar can open on Colonial Avenue without a Conditional Use Permit. That's a $1,080 city fee plus legal consultants, a six-month wait, a public hearing, and a gamble that City Council says yes.

The result is what you see: a neighborhood of extraordinary houses behind dead sidewalks, with the only retail being a 7-Eleven and a Harris Teeter. The pedestrian life that should naturally attach to Ghent's dense, walkable grid simply isn't there — not because residents don't want it, but because the zoning code makes it nearly impossible.

Across town, Colley Avenue and 21st Street are alive. They have Pedestrian Commercial Overlay districts. Their ground floors produce tax revenue, foot traffic, and neighbors who know each other. HC-G2 does not. That's a policy failure, and it's fixable.

What We're Asking For

  • Permit neighborhood-scale retail uses by right on ground floors of parcels with street frontage in HC-G2
  • Use performance standards — not CUPs — to manage scale, hours, and compatibility
  • Preserve ARB review for exterior alterations; don't require it for change of use alone
  • Model the amendment on Norfolk's existing PCO districts, which the city has already proven work
